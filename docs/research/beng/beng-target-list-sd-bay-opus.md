# Bioengineering Target Employer List — San Diego + Bay Area
**For a June 2027 graduate. Built August 2026.**

---

## How to use this

This is a **company list, not a job list.** Most roles relevant to you don't exist yet — spring 2027 requisitions post in spring 2027. The durable value here is knowing *which* companies to watch and *when* each one opens.

Three mechanics that matter more than the list itself:

- **ATS = applicant tracking system**, the software a company posts jobs through (Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, iCIMS, Ashby). Each company's careers page runs on one. Learn to set alerts on the two or three you'll use most.
- **Set job alerts, don't browse.** Every ATS supports saved searches with email alerts. Thirty minutes of setup now beats weekly manual checking for ten months.
- **Verify status before applying.** Biotech consolidates constantly — companies on this list may have been acquired, merged, or shut down since this was written. A quick search on the company name plus "layoffs" or "acquisition" saves wasted applications.

**Tier meanings:**
- **Tier A** — high volume, GPA-insensitive, hires on interviews and availability. Your floor.
- **Tier B** — better pay and prestige, more competitive, more likely to screen on transcript.
- **Tier C** — highest ceiling, credential-indifferent, but demands a specific temperament.

## Research labs — UC San Diego

The fastest possible line on a résumé. Undergrads join by emailing PIs (principal investigators — the professor running the lab) directly. Fall decisions are being made right now.

**How to do it:** Go to the department faculty directory, read three to five recent paper abstracts from labs whose work you can actually describe, and send a short email naming the specific project and what you can contribute. Generic "I'm interested in your research" emails have roughly a 5% response rate; specific ones are closer to 40%. Offer to start as a volunteer if needed — most paid undergrad positions are conversions from unpaid ones.

Travel-heavy, customer-facing, technical. Excellent stepping stone into sales or product management, and hired almost entirely on interview performance.

Roles: Field Service Engineer, Technical Support Engineer, Clinical Applications Specialist, Field Application Scientist.

Best targets: **Illumina, Thermo Fisher, Agilent, Bio-Rad, Cepheid, Roche, 10x Genomics, Intuitive Surgical, Penumbra, ResMed, Dexcom, Olympus, Siemens Healthineers, GE HealthCare, Philips Healthcare, Hologic, Beckman Coulter (Danaher), Sartorius, Bruker, Waters Corporation, PerkinElmer/Revvity.**

# Tier B — R&D and design engineering

The role most people picture when they say "bioengineer." Highest reported satisfaction, most competitive, most likely to screen on transcript at entry. **Apply, but don't build your plan on it.** The reliable route in is two to three years in Tier A at the same company, then an internal transfer — internal candidates face a fraction of the scrutiny.

Best-odds targets are mid-size companies where R&D teams are small enough to hire on portfolio: **Tandem Diabetes, Element Biosciences, Nevro, Procept BioRobotics, Singular Genomics, Alphatec, Bionano, Truvian**, plus any Series B–D device startup.

Large-company R&D programs worth applying to anyway: **Illumina, Intuitive Surgical, Medtronic, Boston Scientific, Abbott, Stryker, Genentech, 10x Genomics.**

**What actually gets you looked at here:** a CAD portfolio, a built prototype, a senior design project you can walk through in mechanical detail, or a lab publication. Any one of these outweighs a GPA point.

# Tier B2 — Bioinformatics & computational biology

$95–130k start, and the pay ceiling is the highest of any non-sales path. Requires real Python, statistics, and command-line fluency — the biology degree alone won't get you in. A master's is common but not required if you have a public GitHub with real analysis work.

**San Diego:** Illumina, Element Biosciences, Singular Genomics, Bionano, Rady Children's Institute for Genomic Medicine, JCVI, SDSC, Scripps Research (translational informatics), Salk (computational biology), Human Longevity, Digital Proteomics, Biolog.

**Bay Area:** 10x Genomics, Guardant Health, Natera, GRAIL, Twist Bioscience, Genentech (computational sciences), Verily, Calico, Insitro, BigHat Biosciences, Chan Zuckerberg Biohub, Gladstone Institutes, Lawrence Berkeley National Lab (Joint Genome Institute), Freenome, Zymergen/Ginkgo.

**How to enter without the credential:** take a public dataset (TCGA, GEO, 1000 Genomes), build a complete analysis pipeline, document it thoroughly, publish it. This is the one track where a portfolio can fully substitute for a transcript.

# Tier B3 — Embedded software & firmware

**The highest-paying engineering path available from a BioE degree** — $100–140k start. The degree is nearly incidental; what matters is C/C++, real-time systems, and signal processing. If you have any programming aptitude at all, this is the highest return on six months of focused effort.

**San Diego:** Dexcom, Tandem Diabetes, ResMed, Illumina (instrument control software), Element Biosciences, Bionano, Qualcomm (health/wearables adjacent).

**Bay Area:** Intuitive Surgical, Penumbra, iRhythm, Verily, Neuralink, Abbott (Alameda — CGM firmware), Boston Scientific, Medtronic (Santa Rosa), Apple (health sensing, Cupertino), Butterfly Network.

**Entry requirement in practice:** two or three embedded projects on GitHub (STM32 or similar microcontroller, a sensor pipeline, an RTOS project) plus demonstrable C. This is achievable between now and spring.

# Tier C — Medical device sales

$80–120k year one, $200–350k+ established, in surgical and cardiac specialties. **Nobody will ask your GPA.** The trade is quota pressure, early-morning operating room cases, and a job that is genuinely social rather than technical. Many engineers hate it; the ones who fit make more than anyone else on this list.

**Standard entry:** Associate Sales Representative or Clinical Specialist, then promotion to full rep territory in 12–24 months.

**Best new-grad programs:**
- **Stryker** — the most famous new-grad sales pipeline in the industry, recruits directly at career fairs
- **Medtronic** — clinical specialist track
- **Boston Scientific** — associate rep program
- **Johnson & Johnson MedTech** (incl. Ethicon, DePuy Synthes)
- **Intuitive Surgical** — clinical sales representative
- **Arthrex** — sports medicine, strong rep culture
- **Zimmer Biomet**, **Smith+Nephew**, **Integra LifeSciences**, **Baxter**, **Olympus**, **Alcon**, **Penumbra**, **Abbott**
- **Independent distributors** — search "[specialty] distributor San Diego"; smaller, faster to hire, less structured

**Adjacent and often overlooked:** life science *tools* sales at Illumina, Thermo Fisher, Agilent, Bio-Rad, 10x Genomics. Less operating-room time, more technical, still strong commission.

# W5 — Post-graduation contract roles

**If June 2027 arrives with nothing lined up, this is the answer — not waiting.** Life sciences staffing agencies place quality, validation, manufacturing, and lab contractors in both metros continuously. They hire on availability rather than transcripts, move from application to start in two to three weeks, and their contracts convert to permanent roles at a high rate.

Contact all of these, not one — they compete for the same reqs and each has different client relationships:

- **Actalent** (the engineering/sciences arm of Aerotek/Allegis) — largest in life sciences
- **Kelly Science & Clinical**
- **Randstad Life Sciences**
- **Planet Pharma**
- **Yoh Life Sciences**
- **Real Staffing** (SThree)
- **ASGN / Lab Support**
- **Insight Global**

**How to work them:** call, don't just apply online. Recruiters place people they've spoken to. Tell one recruiter per agency your availability, salary floor, and that you'll take contract-to-hire — that phrase specifically opens doors that "seeking full-time" closes.

# Application mechanics by window

**W1 (now — September):** Send 15–20 lab emails. Apply to every fall part-time posting you find. Call UCSD's AIP office. Set up job alerts on Workday and Greenhouse for your top 20 companies.

**W2 (October–December):** Attend the Fall Career Fair and the Jacobs School engineering fair with a researched list of 10 companies — check Handshake for the participant list beforehand. Join BMES (Biomedical Engineering Society) and attend its industry nights, where local device companies do informal screening. Apply to structured new-grad programs at Illumina, Genentech, Intuitive, Boston Scientific, Stryker as they open.

**W3 (January–March):** Structured programs close. Apply to spring part-time internships. Contact staffing agencies now to be in their system before you graduate.

**W4 (April–June):** **Volume phase.** This is when most of your applications should go out. Expect to send 100–200 over this window; that is normal, not a sign of failure. Reuse a strong base résumé with targeted tweaks rather than rewriting each one.

**W5 (June onward):** Agencies, direct backfills, and anything that gets you inside a company.

# Two things that matter more than this list

**Referrals beat applications by roughly an order of magnitude.** San Diego medtech runs on them, and UCSD alumni are everywhere in it. Search LinkedIn for "UC San Diego" plus a company name, filter to alumni, and send short informational-chat requests — not job requests. Fifteen minutes of someone's time converts into a referral far more often than you'd expect.

**One completed project beats a transcript.** A built prototype, a documented analysis pipeline, an embedded systems repo, or a Six Sigma Green Belt certification each does more to offset a weak GPA than anything else you can do between now and June. Pick one and finish it.

After your first full-time role, essentially nobody asks about GPA again. The barrier is the first door, not the career.

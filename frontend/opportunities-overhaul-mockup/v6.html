Card layout

The rail, tightened

Two changes. The workplace moves under the location, in a rail that spans both text rows — so it costs no height, and Remote keeps the only green. The rail is now a fixed width instead of a flex edge, which is what fixes the distance: before, a short location like “Austin, TX” was thrown to the far card edge, as far from the company name as the card allowed. Now every location starts at the same x, so it reads as a column with a straight left edge and sits a predictable distance away.

Why 190px is the default. Measured across all 4,534 visible postings with locations capped at two, the string runs 15 characters at the median and 34 at p95. A 190px rail holds 96% of them without truncating; 150px holds about 84%, 230px effectively all of them. The wider you go, the further the location drifts from the company — that is the whole trade.

Left-aligned is the recommendation. It gives the locations a straight left edge, so they form a column you can actually run your eye down — the thing right-alignment cost. Flip to right-aligned to compare; the ragged left edge is what you are looking for.

The rail only reaches into the title row when it has to. Measured, titles run 47 characters at the median and 84 at p90 — so a rail that always spanned both rows would push 32% of titles onto a second line, against 7.7% today. It now spans two rows only on the ~7% of postings that actually carry a workplace; every other card hands the title back the full width. Watch the Cigna title, which has no workplace and stays on one line.

Stress cases at the bottom — a five-city Cigna programme and the Aston Martin posting whose British region resolved to a town in Arkansas. Watch those two when you change the rail width.
